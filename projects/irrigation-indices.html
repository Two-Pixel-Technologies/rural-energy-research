Irrigation Investment Indices

"Irrigation Indices pointed out that farmers need to make Smarter Investments"

Duration: 2021 – 2022
Funding: World Bank & Dept. of Agriculture, Gov. of Maharashtra
Location: Marathwada and Vidarbha, Maharashtra
Status: Completed

Project Overview

Agency: Project on Climate Resilient Agriculture, a project of the Department of Agriculture, Government of Maharashtra and the World Bank.

Irrigation plays a critical role in enhancing agricultural productivity. In Maharashtra, access to irrigation is important due to variability in Monsoon rainfall and to enable rabi cropping.

Farmers are investing in digging wells/borewells and purchasing micro-irrigation equipment, etc., hoping for higher profits through high-valued crops such as sugarcane and horticulture. In this study, We investigated the profitability of different cropping patterns as a function of water sources.

Research Objectives

The project focused on understanding the economics of irrigation investments:

  • Analyze profitability of different cropping patterns based on water sources
  • Develop irrigation investment indices to measure return on investment
  • Study economics across different types of farming systems
  • Provide guidance for smarter irrigation investment decisions
  • Understand relationship between irrigation intensity and returns

Methodology

A study was conducted in the Marathwada and Vidarbha regions to understand the economics of irrigation investment. The irrigation investment index is the ratio of profit considering energy to total irrigation cost.

The study was conducted on farmers in four different villages representative of different types of cropping patterns. This comprehensive approach allowed for a robust analysis across diverse agricultural contexts and water availability scenarios.

Key Findings

The increase in irrigation intensity is accompanied with a mostly decreasing return on investment.

This counterintuitive finding has important implications for farmer decision-making and agricultural policy. While increased irrigation enables more intensive cropping, the costs associated with higher irrigation intensity - including energy costs, equipment, and maintenance - often grow faster than the additional profits generated.

  • Higher irrigation intensity does not always translate to better returns
  • Energy costs play a significant role in irrigation investment economics
  • Optimal irrigation investment varies by cropping pattern and water source
  • Farmers need better information to make investment decisions
  • There is a need for smarter, not just more intensive, irrigation investments

Impact & Implications

The research provides valuable insights for farmers considering irrigation investments. Rather than simply maximizing irrigation capacity, farmers need to carefully evaluate the economics of different investment options considering their specific context, cropping patterns, and water sources.

For policymakers, the findings suggest that programs promoting irrigation expansion should be accompanied by guidance on optimal investment levels and economic viability. Support should focus not just on enabling irrigation access, but on ensuring that irrigation investments are economically sustainable for farmers.

Impact & Outreach

The irrigation investment indices developed in this study provide a practical tool for evaluating irrigation investment decisions. By quantifying the relationship between irrigation intensity and returns, the research helps farmers make more informed choices about where and how much to invest in irrigation infrastructure and equipment.

The study's findings are particularly relevant in the context of government schemes promoting micro-irrigation and well deepening. Understanding the economic returns of different irrigation strategies helps ensure that public investments and farmer resources are directed toward the most beneficial interventions.

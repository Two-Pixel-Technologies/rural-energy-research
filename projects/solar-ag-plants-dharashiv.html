Solar Agricultural Plants in Dharashiv

Duration: August 2025 – Ongoing
Agency: District Collector, Dharashiv
Plant Capacity: 2 – 8 MW scale
Status: Active

Project Overview

The central government is promoting the establishment of 2 - 8 MW scale renewable energy projects connected to Agricultural feeders. This approach results in renewable capacity development with the possibility of providing day time supply to all farmers with potentially lower requirement of distribution/transmission infrastructure enhancement. The scheme is also (a) allowing farmers to be community owners of the projects (b) allows farmers the opportunity to lease/sell agricultural land for a remunerative price.

Reliable and low cost energy, or electrical power, is an important enabler in agricultural, industrial development, and for basic amenities for society. Also, the current renewable energy focus on small / medium sized solar PV and wind power plants is creating opportunities as well as challenges for people in rural areas.

This project conducts the study and analysis of the implementation of the scheme in Dharashiv. The study will focus on project management processes, efficiency, coverage and overall benefits. Other attributes may be added as per discussions with district administration.

Research Objectives

The project focuses on comprehensive evaluation of solar agricultural plant implementation:

  • Study and analyze the implementation of solar Ag feeder schemes in Dharashiv
  • Evaluate project management processes and efficiency
  • Assess coverage and overall benefits to farmers and communities
  • Examine community ownership models and farmer participation
  • Analyze economic impacts including land lease opportunities

Key Research Areas

The research encompasses multiple dimensions of solar agricultural plant implementation:

Project Management and Implementation: Analysis of the processes, timelines, and coordination mechanisms involved in establishing MW-scale solar plants connected to agricultural feeders, identifying best practices and challenges in project execution.

Technical and Operational Efficiency: Evaluation of plant performance, grid integration, daytime power supply quality, and infrastructure requirements, assessing how well the technical objectives are being met.

Social and Economic Benefits: Investigation of community ownership models, farmer participation, economic benefits from land lease arrangements, and overall impact on agricultural livelihoods and rural development.

Scheme Features and Opportunities

The solar agricultural feeder scheme offers multiple benefits:

  • Renewable Capacity Development: Large-scale addition of clean energy capacity connected directly to agricultural feeders
  • Daytime Power Supply: Providing reliable electricity supply to farmers during daytime hours when solar generation is available
  • Reduced Infrastructure Burden: Potentially lower requirements for distribution and transmission infrastructure upgrades
  • Community Ownership: Opportunities for farmers to be community owners of renewable energy projects
  • Land Monetization: Farmers can lease or sell agricultural land for remunerative prices, creating additional income streams

Challenges and Considerations

While the scheme offers significant opportunities, it also presents challenges that require careful study:

The transition to renewable energy infrastructure in rural areas creates both opportunities and challenges for local communities. Understanding the full spectrum of impacts - technical, economic, social, and environmental - is crucial for successful implementation and scaling.

Impact & Outreach

This ongoing study in Dharashiv provides valuable insights into the ground realities of implementing solar agricultural feeder schemes. The findings will inform policymakers, implementation agencies, and district administrations about best practices, challenges, and optimization strategies for such projects.

By documenting the implementation process, assessing benefits and challenges, and evaluating different aspects of the scheme, this research contributes to evidence-based policy development for renewable energy in agriculture. The learnings from Dharashiv can guide similar implementations across India as the country scales up renewable energy capacity for agricultural applications.

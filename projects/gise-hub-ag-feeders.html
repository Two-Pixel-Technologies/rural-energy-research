GIS Representation and Analysis of Water-Energy-Food Linkages in Grid Based Irrigation Systems

Duration: September 2023 – November 2025
Funding: Department of Science and Technology (DST)
Location: Maharashtra
Status: Active

Project Overview

Access to a connection, and quality and schedule of power for irrigation is a major problem for farmers. Socio-political issues in this sector have led to a system with poor quality supply for farmers, and losses for distribution utilities. With climate change and unpredictable rainfall, protective irrigation is becoming imperative for all farmers.

Solutions to address issues are being sought through newer technical paradigms such as offgrid solar photovoltaic pumps, High Voltage Distribution Systems (HVDS) etc. On the other hand, balancing the conflicting objectives of reducing irrigation water and energy usage, increasing food security, and farmer income, have become very important in India. Effective policy design, as well as promoting operational efficiency in the system, requires analysis through systems representing water energy-food linkages at the local level.

This project addresses this need by documenting the electrical distribution system of agricultural feeders in GIS and connecting it to the cropping patterns and water sources. Thus setting up a system which may be used for design and analysis while optimizing resources and farmer requirements.

Research Objectives

The project focuses on developing a comprehensive GIS-based analytical framework:

  • Document electrical distribution systems of agricultural feeders in GIS
  • Connect distribution infrastructure data with cropping patterns and water sources
  • Enable design and analysis optimizing resources and farmer requirements
  • Support effective policy design for water-energy-food nexus issues
  • Improve operational efficiency in irrigation electricity systems

Key Research Areas

The research encompasses several interconnected components:

GIS Documentation: Comprehensive mapping and documentation of electrical distribution networks for agricultural feeders, including line configurations, transformer locations, pump connections, and network topology in a Geographic Information System framework.

Water-Energy-Food Linkages: Integration of agricultural data including cropping patterns, water source types, irrigation practices, and crop water requirements with the electrical distribution network data to create a comprehensive analytical system.

System Analysis and Optimization: Development of analytical tools and methods to optimize infrastructure design, load management, and resource allocation while balancing farmer needs, system efficiency, and sustainability objectives.

Talegaon Feeder, Wardha - GIS representation showing electrical network and agricultural connections

Applications

The GIS-based system developed through this project enables multiple applications:

  • Infrastructure planning and design optimization
  • Load forecasting and capacity planning
  • Policy analysis for water-energy-food nexus issues
  • Assessment of technical paradigms (solar pumps, HVDS, etc.)
  • Operational efficiency improvements for distribution utilities

Impact & Outreach

This project creates a foundational system for evidence-based planning and policy-making at the intersection of water, energy, and agriculture. By integrating electrical distribution infrastructure with agricultural data in a GIS framework, it enables sophisticated analysis that was previously not possible.

The system supports both immediate operational decisions (like load management and infrastructure maintenance) and long-term strategic planning (like evaluating new technical paradigms and designing policies for sustainable resource use). The GIS-based approach ensures that analysis is grounded in spatial realities and can account for local variations in infrastructure, cropping patterns, and water availability.

Reports and code developed under this project have been submitted to DST, with presentations made at DST review meetings demonstrating the system's capabilities and potential applications.
